Position

Nobody knows the current state of the art for geospatial foundation models.

Papers in the audited corpus do not share an evaluation framework, they report different numbers for the same model on the same benchmark, and they change architecture and pretraining data together without ablations that separate the two. We think this is a coordination failure and not the fault of any one lab, and the authors of this paper have contributed to it.

Findings

Five trends in the 152-paper corpus

T1 Model weights are not released

Of the 152 papers, 59 (39%) release no model weights. Twenty-six say so explicitly, and we could not find weights for the other 33. Twenty-nine of the 59 provide public code but no weights, so reusing those models requires retraining them.

T2 No shared core of benchmarks

The papers evaluate on 401 distinct benchmarks in 1,046 evaluations. EuroSAT, NWPU-RESISC45, and AID are the most used, yet together they account for 10.6% of evaluations, and 50 of 143 papers (35%) use none of the ten most common benchmarks. The Gini coefficient of benchmark usage has been stable since 2023, while the number of benchmarks used by a single paper grew from 13 in 2022 to 98 in 2025.

T3 Reported numbers disagree across papers

We grouped the 11,488 reported results by model, benchmark, metric, evaluation protocol, and training regime. After excluding generic baselines and detection benchmarks, 252 groups are reported by at least two papers. The reported values differ by at least 5 points in 47 of them (18.7%), by at least 10 in 18, and by at least 20 in 6. For example, CROMA's mIoU on m-Cashew-Plantation is 30.1 in DOFA and 62.2 in SatDiFuser, and the papers do not give enough detail to tell whether the decoders and evaluation settings were the same. The median spread is 0.1 points and the 90th percentile is 8.3.

T4 Dataset bundles without a shared harness

GEO-Bench, PANGAEA, FoMo-Bench, and PhilEO Bench provide curated datasets and splits, but each researcher still writes their own training and evaluation code, so the optimizer, learning rate, evaluation crop, and IoU averaging vary between papers that use the same dataset. The field has no counterpart to lm-evaluation-harness, a versioned tool that every model owner runs to produce a reported number.

T5 Architecture confounded with pretraining data

The 126 papers that name a pretraining dataset use 87 distinct datasets, and the most common, MillionAID, appears in 9 papers. Only 32 papers (25%) pretrain on the same set of datasets as any other paper, and the other 94 each use a unique set. When a paper changes both the model and the data without holding one fixed, readers cannot tell which change produced the gain.

Recommendations

Six recommendations for authors, reviewers, and venues

The paper also gives a reviewer checklist (C1–C6) that maps to these.

  1. R1

    Release weights under a named license

    Release them by camera-ready, or state the constraint that prevents release.

  2. R2

    Report on a shared core of benchmarks

    Use core benchmarks that fit the model's modality and claimed tasks, and state the protocol.

  3. R3

    Mark each baseline as copied or rerun

    Copied rows cite the source paper and its protocol, and rerun rows give the configuration.

  4. R4

    Report seed variability

    Give mean ± std over at least three runs where affordable, and otherwise say the result is from one run.

  5. R5

    Build a shared evaluation harness

    A third party maintains one tool with versioned protocols and automated submission checks.

  6. R6

    Separate data from architecture and algorithm

    When more than one changes, include a controlled run on a public pretraining dataset.

What we did

We read 152 GFM papers from 2019–2025 and extracted 11,488 reported results, and tested whether results from different papers can be compared.

What we found

39% of papers have no public weights, so their results cannot be reproduced.
released 93 not released 26 not found 33
401 benchmarks across 152 papers. 35% of the papers that run evaluations use none of the ten most common.
94/126 papers pretrain on a dataset combination that no other paper uses, so model gains cannot be separated from data gains.

Nobody knows which geospatial foundation model is best.

CROMA scores 30.1 mIoU on m-Cashew in one paper and 62.2 in another. Of the 252 results that more than one paper reports under the same protocol, 47 differ by 5 points or more.

We have published tables like these ourselves, and we think fixing them takes shared benchmarks and tooling that no single lab can provide.

What we ask of every GFM paper

  1. R1
    Release the weights under a named license, or say what prevents it.
  2. R2
    Evaluate on a shared core of benchmarks and state the protocol.
  3. R3
    Label every baseline as ↻ rerun or ⧉ copied, with its source.
  4. R4
    Report seed variance, or say the result is from one run.
  5. R5
    Build one shared harness with versioned protocols that everyone runs.
  6. R6
    Separate data from model. If both change, add one run on a public pretraining set.

For reviewers

A paper need not satisfy all six, but ask whether the ones it skips could explain the claimed gain.

Come argue with us

We do not yet know which benchmarks belong in the shared core or who should run the harness, and we want your opinion on both.
